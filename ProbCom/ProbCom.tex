\chapter*{Preface}\label{ch:preface}

These are my notes for \emph{Probabilistic Combinatorics} (MATH 5390), taught by
Shagnik Das at National Taiwan University in Fall 2026.

The course is about the \emph{probabilistic method} in extremal combinatorics.
The idea is simple: to show that a structure with some desired property exists,
build a probability space over all candidate structures and prove that a random
one has the property with positive probability. Such an argument rarely says
\emph{which} structure works, only that one must; yet with sharper tools from
probability theory it becomes remarkably powerful, and it has settled countless
problems in combinatorics. Rather than develop the probability first, the course
builds the toolkit gradually, introducing each technique through applications
from different corners of combinatorics. Roughly, the topics fall into three
groups.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Basic probabilistic method}: quick applications, linearity of
    expectation, the method of alterations, the second moment method, and the
    R\"odl nibble.
    \item \textbf{Advanced methods}: the Lov\'asz Local Lemma, martingales and
    concentration inequalities, Janson's inequalities, and quasirandomness.
    \item \textbf{Modern advances} (time permitting): hypergraph containers,
    spread distributions and sunflowers, and fractional versus expectation
    thresholds.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Prerequisites.}
The course assumes Combinatorics I and II or Graph Theory I and II, together
with undergraduate probability and calculus.

\paragraph{Sources.}
Most of the course follows Alon and Spencer, \emph{The Probabilistic Method}.
These notes are based on the lectures; any errors in them are mine.

\paragraph{Assessment.}
Homework is assigned every two weeks and done in pairs. There is a closed-book
midterm (Week~8) and final (Week~16), each asking both for definitions,
statements and proofs of theorems from lectures and for solutions to problems
in the style of the homework. The grade is the higher of
\(30\%\) homework \(+\) \(30\%\) midterm \(+\) \(40\%\) final and
\(30\%\) homework \(+\) \(70\%\) final.
